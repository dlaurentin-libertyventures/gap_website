Want real change in higher education? Start with accreditation.

Accreditation decides which institutions can exist, grant degrees, and reach federal aid. Right now it rewards documentation and protects incumbents, and it says almost nothing about whether the education itself is any good.

the problem

A gatekeeper that measures the wrong things.

The current system asks institutions to prove they followed a long list of procedures. It tells you little about whether teaching and learning are actually healthy.

Serious new institutions with different missions face years of expensive, uncertain review. Established ones rarely face consequences when the education they offer goes bad.

Hard to enter, slow to remove failure, and weak at telling anyone what real quality looks like.

the global aim

Global is earned, not declared.

Accreditation and quality assurance are nationally situated. Recognition is granted by national authorities, not declared by a global body. So we are building GAP in the United States first, and seeking recognition from the U.S. Department of Education at a moment when the regulatory environment is open to a different model.

Recognition here is the foundation, not the ceiling. Once established, we will build toward mutual recognition with quality assurance agencies and education ministries abroad, so that an institution judged sound by GAP carries a signal that travels.

Global is the direction. We earn it through national credibility rather than claim it ahead of the work.

GAP is an independent initiative building a new accreditor, one designed to recognize institutions for the health of their educational practice rather than the thickness of their compliance files.
