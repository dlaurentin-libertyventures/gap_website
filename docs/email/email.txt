Hi, Chad --

Thanks to you and Deb for making this website a reality. I'm copying Lori so she is aware of the changes I'm suggesting, and why. 

I reworked the homepage draft before it goes any further. A few lines were quietly fighting our own framework, and one of them was the word in our name. If we posted the draft as written, the site would contradict the standards the moment anyone read past the opener. Here is what moved and why, so we can hold the line whenever it comes up again:

First, I cut "Outcomes Over Inputs: evaluate student learning, completion, and long-term success." That is the position Standard Three exists to argue against. Our whole claim is that you cannot read the health of teaching off completion rates and earnings data. Putting that promise on the front page hands every future critic the contradiction they need for free. It now reads "Judge the practice, not the paperwork."

Second, I cut "Lean but Effective: Reduce institutional burden." This is the one promise we cannot keep honestly. The practice-ordered model asks for more judgment, not less work. Real review of a practice is more demanding than filling binders, not less. We will stop demanding documentation that tells no one anything. So the line is now "Demanding where it counts, quiet where it doesn’t.” We aim to reduce unnecessary work. Anything other than that promise puts us squarely at odds with ourselves and a future NACIQI.

Third, I pulled the innovation framing that ran through the whole draft. "Choke-point for innovation," "incumbency over innovation," "constraining innovation." All of that lives inside the paradigm we are built to leave. Borrowing this banner concedes the argument before we open our mouths. The problem section now leads with the gatekeeper measuring the wrong things, which is our argument. 

Fourth, and this is the one that keeps surfacing, I added a short section that says plainly what makes us "global." The original instinct to build a global accreditor from the ground up was misguided, as accreditation and quality assurance are nationally situated. National authorities grant recognition. Nobody just declares itself global, and is believed. 

So the honest sequence here is the reverse of how it has read: We build in the United States first and seek Department of Education recognition at an open moment, and that recognition becomes the asset we carry into every later conversation with a foreign ministry or quality assurance agency. National credibility is not a detour from the global aim. It is the precondition for it. The new section says exactly that, and the page no longer calls us "international" before we have earned the word properly.

The simple version of what we can and cannot promise:

We can provide a review that fits an institution's mission, a fair and staged route in for serious new entrants, an end to box-ticking that informs no one, an honest judgment about whether the educational practice is sound, and a path toward recognition abroad once we are established at home.

We cannot promise less work, because the model is designed to be heavier on judgment. We cannot promise to score institutions on learning and completion metrics, because that is the system we are leaving. We cannot wave the innovation flag without giving away the ground that makes us different. And we cannot call ourselves global before a single national authority has recognized us, because the honest answer to "global by what authority?" is, for now, none yet.

I also changed the closing line from "recognizes institutions" to "designed to recognize," so the site speaks as the incubator we are rather than the accreditor we are building. Small phrase, but the same fault line as the global question.

Drafts attached. Happy to walk through any of it.

Cheers,
Gerry
